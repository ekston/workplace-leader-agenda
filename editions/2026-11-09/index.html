01
The Big Trade-Off

The ‘agentic sprawl’
vs. centralised governance trap.

Published retroactively on 16 November 2026.

The Tension

Business units are building their own Copilot Studio agents and LLM workflows, mostly because that's quicker than waiting for IT. It's good for speed. It also creates non-human identities nobody tracks, and agents that reach into SharePoint through API calls nobody reviewed. Microsoft's own documentation says Conditional Access doesn't apply when an agent uses an API key, and that policies aimed at 'all users' don't cover agent user accounts. For FINMA-regulated firms there's a second problem. Circular 2023/1 expects a complete, current inventory of ICT assets, and an agent nobody registered isn't in it.

The Leadership Call

Do we lock down agent creation centrally and accept more shadow AI, plus some grumbling in the business units? Or do we build a governed sandbox where business units can still create agents quickly, inside automatic guardrails?

The Decision

Freeze ad-hoc agent publishing now. By the end of the month, every autonomous workflow in the tenant should draw on a governed Copilot Credit budget and run under explicit Entra Conditional Access scopes. Teams that want to build can keep building, in the sandbox.

The Reality Check

Since 1 July 2026, Microsoft 365 E3 lists at $39 and E5 at $60 per user per month, and the Microsoft 365 Copilot add-on costs another $30. Agents and other metered features then use Copilot Credits, either pay-as-you-go at $0.01 each or from a prepaid annual pool bought at a 5–20% discount. So at Microsoft's USD list prices a Copilot user costs about $69 to $90 a month before the first credit is spent. Swiss contract prices will differ.

The Leadership Action

Pull the Copilot usage report and look at weekly active use per paid seat. Independent write-ups put weekly use at 20–30% of paid seats, but that's a secondary estimate, so trust your own numbers first. Before the next billing cycle, move idle licences from low-use groups to roles that actually use them, such as analysts and engineers.

The Reality Check

Copilot only shows people what they already have access to, so the weak spot is the permissions underneath. Semantic search makes it easy to find old HR files, salary spreadsheets and forgotten project folders on flat SharePoint sites that were technically open all along. Under the nFADP, salary data open to the whole company was already a data security problem. Copilot just makes it easier to notice.

The Leadership Action

Ask SecOps and Information Protection to start with the weekly data risk assessment that Purview DSPM for AI already runs on your top 100 SharePoint sites. Fix stale and unmanaged permissions first, then apply sensitivity labels to historical repositories. Locking down Copilot itself achieves little while the permissions stay wide open.

Faster attacks, fragile updates

Sources
Google Threat Intelligence ↗The Record ↗KB5127648 ↗Microsoft Q&A (community) ↗Tech Community (community) ↗
The Reality Check

Endpoint teams are squeezed from two sides. Google reports that vulnerability disclosures have doubled to about 10,000 a month, with AI helping, and that attackers now exploit known flaws faster. Meanwhile the October cumulative update KB5127648 brought its own trouble. Admins report boot failures on some Dell devices, which Microsoft hasn't listed as a known issue. Microsoft does document a Remote Desktop error for non-admin users and a stricter NTLMv1 default that can affect Wi-Fi, Ethernet and VPN connections using MS-CHAPv2.

The Leadership Action

Patching doesn't have to be all-or-nothing. Check that your pilot ring contains the hardware you really run, including Dell models and VDI hosts, so it finds regressions before everyone else does. A ring made only of IT's own laptops mostly tests IT's own laptops.

Adoption, habits and trust
Pillar 04 / 04
This week

Beyond the licence count

Sources
Usage estimate (weak) ↗Copilot pricing ↗
The Reality Check

Buying seats is the easy part. In many organisations middle managers don't yet trust automated summaries, and few teams have agreed how they'd measure a redesigned process, so adoption stalls after the launch email. The weekly-use estimate in Pillar 1 points the same way.

The Leadership Action

Report success as cycle-time reduction in a few chosen processes, such as customer onboarding or compliance reporting, and drop 'licences assigned' as the headline number. Peer champions who show their own work will do more than another generic how-to video.

03
Weekly Executive Action List

The 3 calls to make.

01 / 03
Call with CISO / Security Architect

Check that controls for non-human identities and agent-targeted Conditional Access are really switched on, so data can't leave through third-party plugins nobody monitors. If you use Agent 365, its agent IDs and policy templates are worth a look.

02 / 03
Call with Finance / Procurement Lead

Look at the Azure meter for Copilot Credits together. Agent workloads are spiky, and a spiky meter without a spending limit tends to show up in the budget review.

03 / 03
Call with DEX / Endpoint Lead

Did the last cumulative update reach the pilot ring's Dell devices and VDI hosts before it reached everyone else? If the answer is 'we'll check', check this week.

04
The Anti-Pattern · What to stop doing this week
Stop measuring AI success by deployment scale.

Handing out licences to hit an adoption quota, without tying people to specific workflows, buys expensive shelfware. Put governance and training effort where you can measure cycle time going down.

Method

Phillip Büchler's draft of 9 November 2026, rewritten and fact-checked on 16 November 2026 using only sources published by 9 November 2026. Prices are Microsoft's USD list prices; we found no Swiss franc list price to quote. The draft's '$66 to $90+' used the old E3 price and is corrected to about $69 to $90. Its '20–30% active daily usage' is reported as weekly use of paid seats, from a secondary write-up (weak source). The 'surge in zero-days through late 2026' is replaced by Google's data on doubled disclosures and faster exploitation of known flaws, because Google counts zero-days rising only from 8 to 11 a month. 'Agent Guardrails' isn't a Microsoft product name, so the edition describes Agent 365's controls instead. The spending-policy documentation we cite was last updated on 3 November; we couldn't confirm when credit pools and spending policies first appeared in tenants. Middle-management resistance in Pillar 4 is the author's assessment, not a sourced figure. Dell boot failures after KB5127648 are community reports, not a Microsoft known issue.
